Job Market Paper

The China Shock and Death on American Roads

PDF Abstract

I estimate how the persistent labor demand shock caused by rising Chinese import competition affected risky driving on U.S. roads and the deaths of other road users. Using the federal record of every fatal crash from 1991 to 2019, I find that commuting zones more exposed to import competition had about 9 percent more working-age risky drivers involved in fatal crashes than less exposed zones in the same state. I also find a 14 percent increase in the deaths of other people in the crashes that involve a working-age risky driver. The estimate implies approximately 460 additional deaths annually between 2001 and 2019 attributable to the rise in import competition. Using the value of a statistical life, I estimate the annual value of the deaths of other people at approximately $5.0 billion. These findings point to a potential external cost of trade-induced labor market disruption that could reduce the net gains from trade.
