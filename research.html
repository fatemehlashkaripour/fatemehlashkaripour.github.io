Research

Job Market Paper

Import Competition, Impaired Driving, and Harm to Others

Earlier title: Driving through Decline: Trade Exposure and Alcohol-Involved Fatal Crashes

PDF Abstract

I estimate the effect of rising Chinese import competition on alcohol impaired driving in U.S. local labor markets and on the deaths of other road users. Using the federal record of every fatal crash on U.S. roads from 1991 to 2019, I find that commuting zones more exposed to import competition had about 13 percent more working-age drivers in fatal crashes at or above the legal alcohol limit than less exposed zones in the same state. The increase is not explained by more alcohol testing. It appears among both men and women, though men account for about three quarters of the additional impaired drivers. It is not detectable in the 1990s, emerges after 2000, is largest after 2008, and persists through 2019. Unlike recessions, which are associated with fewer traffic deaths, regionally concentrated and persistent job loss makes roads more dangerous. The harm extends beyond the drivers themselves; deaths of people in other vehicles rise by about 9 percent, roughly 430 deaths a year when scaled to the nation. Valued at about 4.7 billion dollars a year, these deaths are a cost of job loss that firms do not pay and that drivers pay only in small part.

Storms of Change: Exploring the Impact of Hurricanes on Substance Use

A hurricane, like a permanent job loss, is a shock people do not choose. This paper studies how substance use changes in the places hurricanes hit, asking how people adjust risky behavior after a shock in a new setting.

Publications

Reduction in the Introduction of Complementary Foods Prior to 4 Months and Prior to 6 Months: An Examination of the U.S. National Survey of Children’s Health 2016–2023

(Breastfeeding Medicine, 2025 – with Angela G. Campbell)

Journal

Using the National Survey of Children’s Health, 2016–2023, we study when infants are first given complementary foods (foods other than breast milk or formula) and show that fewer infants received them before 4 months and before 6 months over this period.
